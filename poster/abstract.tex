\title{Symmetry-Constrained Discovery of Subgrid Transport in Rayleigh--B\'enard Convection}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
In Rayleigh--B\'enard convection, temperature differences drive fluid motion, which in turn transports heat. Simulations that resolve only the largest scales must therefore account for both momentum and heat transport by unresolved motions. Eddy-viscosity and eddy-diffusivity closures prescribe stress--strain and flux--gradient alignment, restricting the interactions they can represent.

We examine how resolved velocity and buoyancy gradients encode subgrid transport in two-dimensional convection. Using filtered direct numerical simulation data, we apply symmetry-constrained equation discovery to infer interpretable expressions for the subgrid stress and buoyancy flux without imposing these alignment assumptions. The constraints enforce the tensor transformation properties of the candidate terms.

Preliminary results recover nonlinear-gradient terms for both the subgrid stress and buoyancy flux. These terms describe products of resolved-field gradients, identifying contributions to momentum and heat transport that are not restricted to the alignment assumed in scalar eddy-viscosity and eddy-diffusivity models.
\end{abstract}
