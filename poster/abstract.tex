\title{Data-Driven Discovery of Subgrid Closures for Rayleigh--B\'enard Convection}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Buoyancy-driven turbulence transports heat in systems ranging from laboratory convection to geophysical flows. Resolving every turbulent scale is often impractical, motivating models of how unresolved motions influence the resolved flow. For thermal convection, such models must describe both momentum and buoyancy transport.

We investigate interpretable subgrid closures in two-dimensional Rayleigh--B\'enard convection using symmetry-constrained sparse regression. Using filtered velocity and buoyancy fields from direct numerical simulations, we seek compact expressions for the unresolved stress and buoyancy flux. We aim to assess these models against nonlinear gradient expansions through closure accuracy and local and mean kinetic-energy and scalar-variance transfer across filter widths. We also investigate how uniform-grid interpolation and integration on native Chebyshev nodes affect weak-form equation recovery.

The goal is to develop physically interpretable models of unresolved momentum and heat transport for large-eddy simulations of buoyancy-driven flows, while identifying the strengths and limitations of gradient-based closures.
\end{abstract}
