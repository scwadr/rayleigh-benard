\title{Data-Driven Discovery of Subgrid Closures for Rayleigh--B\'enard Convection}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Buoyancy-driven turbulence transports heat in systems ranging from laboratory convection to geophysical flows. Resolving every turbulent scale is often impractical, motivating models of how unresolved motions influence the resolved flow. For thermal convection, such models must describe both momentum and buoyancy transport.

We present a framework for discovering interpretable subgrid closures in two-dimensional Rayleigh--B\'enard convection using symmetry-constrained sparse regression. Starting from direct numerical simulations, we filter the velocity and buoyancy fields and identify compact expressions for the unresolved stress and buoyancy flux. We assess the resulting models against nonlinear gradient expansions, examining closure accuracy and their predictions of local and mean kinetic-energy and scalar-variance transfer across filter widths. We also investigate the treatment of nonuniform vertical sampling in weak-form equation recovery, comparing interpolation onto a uniform grid with integration on native Chebyshev nodes.

This approach links data-driven equation discovery to physically interpretable models of unresolved transport and provides a way to assess the strengths and limitations of gradient-based closures in buoyancy-driven turbulence.
\end{abstract}
